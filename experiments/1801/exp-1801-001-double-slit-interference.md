# EXP-1801-001: Double-Slit Light Interference

## Hypothesis

If light is composed of particles (as proposed by Isaac Newton), passing a beam of light through two parallel slits will result in two distinct lines of light on a screen behind them. If light propagates as a wave, the overlapping waves from the two slits will interfere with each other, creating a pattern of multiple alternating bright and dark bands (an interference pattern).

## Method

1.  **Setup**: A dark room with a single small source of sunlight (a pinhole in a window covering).
2.  **Apparatus**: A thin card is used to split the beam of light. (Effectively creating two narrow, closely spaced slits).
3.  **Measurement**: Observe the pattern cast on a screen placed at a distance behind the slits.
4.  **Control**: Cover one slit to ensure a single slit produces a single band of light.

## Results

When light passed through both slits simultaneously, the screen did not show just two lines. Instead, a series of alternating bright and dark fringes appeared. The brightest fringe was in the direct center (where a shadow would be expected if light were purely particles moving in straight lines).

## Conclusions

The results definitively contradict the pure corpuscular (particle) theory of light. The alternating bright and dark bands can only be explained by constructive and destructive interference of waves. Therefore, light must behave as a wave.

- This fundamentally shifts our mental model of optics from Newtonian particles to wave mechanics.
- Follow-up questions: Through what medium do these waves propagate? (Luminiferous aether?)
